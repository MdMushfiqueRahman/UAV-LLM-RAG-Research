## 3. Reasoning

**Evidence that could favor Malicious:**
- Three features move together in a direction often associated with GNSS interference: the jamming indicator rises, satellite count falls, and reported velocity rises.
- Reported velocity grows steadily while recorded position barely changes. This could indicate a mismatch between the velocity solution and the position solution.

**Evidence that could favor Benign:**
- Fix type stays 3 throughout.
- Satellite count stays at about 7.7 or higher, which is generally adequate for a 3D solution.
- The jamming indicator changes only modestly, and there is no reference for what counts as elevated.
- Position and altitude are smooth, with no jumps, teleports or abrupt resets.

**Why I am not choosing either:**

The features that would separate the two hypotheses depend on information that is not supplied. Here, the missing information materially affects the conclusion.

1. **Velocity versus position consistency depends on units.**
   - If timestamps were microseconds, the window would be about 0.35 s. Speeds of 1.25–2.25 would then imply roughly 0.6 m of travel, against about 2 cm observed. That would be a real inconsistency.
   - If timestamps are in a finer unit, the two could be consistent.
   - It is also unclear whether vel_m_s is horizontal speed, 3D speed, or something else.
   - I cannot establish the mismatch without assuming units, so I do not treat it as confirmed.

2. **The jamming indicator's significance is unknown.**
   - A move from 41.1 to 42.0 may be noise-level or meaningful.
   - There is no scale, threshold or pre-window baseline to judge it.

3. **The data appear interpolated or synthetic.**
   - Velocity, jamming and satellites all change by perfectly constant increments.
   - These increments do not depend on the irregular time spacing.
   - Satellite counts are fractional.
   - Altitude has a clean breakpoint at record 5.
   - These traits suggest resampling or linear interpolation between anchor points. The window may therefore reflect processing artifacts rather than raw sensor dynamics. That weakens any inference drawn from the trends.

Taken together, the observations are compatible with both a mild degradation or interference scenario and a normal, nearly stationary hover with interpolated telemetry. The data do not meaningfully favor one.

## 4. Alternative explanations

- **Normal satellite geometry changes:** satellites setting below the horizon or being masked could reduce the count gradually without any attack.
- **Benign noise-floor variation:** the jamming indicator can drift with ambient RF conditions, onboard electronics or motor load. A rise of about 0.9 may be within normal variation.
- **Velocity definition or estimator behavior:** the velocity field may be estimator-derived, smoothed, or a different quantity from what position differencing captures, such as 3D speed or a filtered estimate.
- **Data-processing artifacts:** linear interpolation, resampling or merging of logs at different rates could produce the constant increments, fractional satellite counts and altitude breakpoint.
- **Near-stationary hover:** the position and altitude behavior is consistent with a vehicle holding position.

## 5. Confidence: **Medium**

I am fairly confident the window does not, by itself, distinguish the hypotheses. However, if the time units were confirmed as microseconds, the velocity–position mismatch would shift the assessment toward Malicious.

## 6. Additional evidence needed

- **Units and definitions:** timestamp units, the lat/lon format, the scale and normal range of the jamming indicator, and what vel_m_s measures (horizontal, 3D, estimator output, or GNSS-reported).
- **Baseline windows:** telemetry before and after this window, to see whether the trends continue, reverse, or exceed normal ranges.
- **Raw data:** non-interpolated satellite counts and raw sample timing, to rule out processing artifacts.
- **Additional GNSS quality metrics:** HDOP/VDOP, eph/epv, C/N0 per satellite, noise per millisecond, and spoofing or AGC flags.
- **Cross-sensor checks:** IMU accelerations, optical flow, barometer and the commanded flight mode. These would test whether the rising reported velocity is physically real or inconsistent with the vehicle's actual motion.
