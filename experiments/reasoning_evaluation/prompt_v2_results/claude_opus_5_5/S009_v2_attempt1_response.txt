# Telemetry Classification: Sample S009

## 1. Classification
**Benign**

## 2. Supporting telemetry observations

**Position (lat_x, lon_x)**
- lon_x is constant at 138.2529156 across all 10 records.
- lat_x is 36.20482055 for records 1–8 and 36.20482056 for records 9–10. That is a single change in the last reported digit (1e-8).
- There are no jumps, oscillations or discontinuities in position.

**Altitude (alt_x)**
- Range is 51.93466 to 51.938145, a total spread of about 0.0035.
- Records 2–8 rise in nearly identical steps of about 0.00058 each.
- Records 9–10 decline in steps of about 0.00032.
- The trend is smooth and small, with no abrupt changes.

**Satellites and fix**
- satellites_used is constant at 11.0.
- fix_type is constant at 3.
- Neither degrades at any point in the window.

**jamming_indicator**
- Records 1–5 fall slightly and linearly from 21.129 to 21.0, in steps of about −0.0323.
- Records 6–10 rise linearly from 21.655 to 24.276, in steps of about +0.655.
- Net change is about +3.15, roughly a 15% increase over the window.

**vel_m_s**
- Records 1–5 rise slightly, from 0.07735 to 0.078.
- Records 6–10 fall linearly, from 0.07645 to 0.07024.
- All values are small and close to each other.

**Timestamps**
- Intervals are irregular: about 45k, 5k, 50k, 28k, 27k, 45k, 45k, 5k and 50k.
- Units are not stated.

**Cross-feature relationships**
- The jamming_indicator rise in records 6–10 coincides with a slight velocity decrease and a small altitude change.
- It does not coincide with any change in satellites_used, fix_type or position.

## 3. Reasoning

**Evidence for Benign**
- The navigation solution appears stable across every quality-related field: constant satellite count, constant fix type and essentially unchanged position.
- Low, slowly varying velocity and millimeter-scale altitude variation are internally consistent with a stationary or near-stationary vehicle. However, units are not given, so any physical interpretation is tentative.
- Nothing shows the signatures commonly associated with spoofing, such as:
  - position jumps or steady drift inconsistent with velocity,
  - altitude discontinuities,
  - sudden changes in satellite count.
- Nothing shows the degradation commonly associated with effective jamming, such as falling satellites_used or a downgraded fix_type.

**Evidence for Malicious**
- The jamming_indicator rises steadily and monotonically in the second half of the window, at a rate about 20 times faster than its earlier decline.
- Read in isolation, this could be the early onset of interference.
- However, it is a single feature with a modest change, and the window has no baseline to say whether values in the 21–24 range are normal for this platform.
- No other feature corroborates an effect on the GNSS solution.
- Per the guidance, a single changing feature, especially one interpreted from its name alone, is not sufficient to support a malicious classification.

**Why not Insufficient Evidence**
- The window does contain meaningful, specific information: several independent indicators of navigation integrity all remain stable, and they agree with each other.
- The missing baseline limits interpretation of the jamming_indicator's absolute level.
- It does not prevent assessing the coherence of the overall telemetry, which favors Benign.

**Observation about data structure**
- The very regular, piecewise-linear steps in alt_x, jamming_indicator and vel_m_s suggest the values may have been resampled or interpolated.
- The jamming steps are close to 1/31 and 19/29, and the trend direction changes at records 5–6.
- This is a possible explanation, not an established fact. It does not by itself indicate either class.

## 4. Alternative explanations

**Possible benign causes of the rising jamming_indicator**
- Ordinary changes in the RF environment, such as nearby electronics or onboard emissions.
- Changes in antenna orientation or the vehicle's own electrical load.
- Normal fluctuation of the indicator over time.

**Possible explanations for the smooth trends**
- Data-processing artifacts such as interpolation, resampling or log alignment. This could explain the linear patterns and the slope change between records 5 and 6.

**Possible explanations for the small velocity and altitude variation**
- Normal estimator noise or slight hovering motion.

**Malicious possibilities that cannot be fully excluded**
- Low-power interference whose effect has not yet reached satellites_used or fix_type.
- A sophisticated spoofing scenario designed to keep position consistent. This window offers no positive evidence for it.

## 5. Confidence
**Medium**

- The stability of position, satellites and fix across all records gives meaningful support for Benign.
- Confidence is not High because:
  - the jamming_indicator trend is unexplained,
  - there is no baseline,
  - units and feature definitions are not provided,
  - the window is short.

## 6. Additional evidence needed

- **Baseline jamming_indicator values** for this platform and environment, to judge whether 21–24 is typical. It would also help to know the indicator's scale and its threshold for concern.
- **Records after this window**, to see whether the jamming_indicator keeps rising and whether satellites_used, fix_type or position eventually degrade.
- **Signal-quality fields**, such as carrier-to-noise density per satellite, noise level, AGC, spoofing flags, HDOP/VDOP and position accuracy estimates.
- **Independent navigation sources**, such as IMU or inertial dead-reckoning, barometric altitude, optical flow or visual odometry, to cross-check the GNSS position and velocity.
- **Feature definitions and units** for timestamps, velocity, altitude and jamming_indicator.
- **Data-processing documentation**, to confirm whether values were interpolated or resampled.
- **Mission context**, such as whether the vehicle was intended to be hovering or stationary.
