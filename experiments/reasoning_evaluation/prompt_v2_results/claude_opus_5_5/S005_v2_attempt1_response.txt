# Telemetry Analysis: Sample S005

## 1. Classification: **Benign**

## 2. Supporting telemetry observations

**Receiver status (stable throughout all 10 records)**
- `satellites_used` stays at 11.0 in every record.
- `fix_type` stays at 3 in every record.

**Position (very small, smooth changes)**
- `lat_x` falls from 36.20481883 to 36.20481841, a total change of about 4.2×10⁻⁷.
- Per-record latitude steps are −1, −1, −4, −5, −4, −4, −4, −10 and −9 (×10⁻⁸). The changes are small and monotonic, with a modest increase in step size near the end.
- `lon_x` rises from 138.2529198 to 138.2529203, changing only in the 7th decimal place.
- No jumps, reversals or discontinuities appear in either coordinate.

**Altitude (smooth, piecewise-linear rise)**
- `alt_x` rises from 51.778516 to 51.8244576, a total of about 0.046.
- The step sizes fall into three constant segments:
  - 0.004902 for steps 1–2
  - 0.00538 for steps 3–7
  - 0.0046188 for steps 8–9

**Jamming indicator (exactly linear decrease)**
- `jamming_indicator` falls from 27.69565217 to 24.95652174.
- It decreases by a constant ≈0.30434783 per record.

**Velocity (exactly linear decrease)**
- `vel_m_s` falls from 0.07534783 to 0.06947827.
- It decreases by a constant ≈0.00065217 per record.
- Both this series and the jamming indicator change by a fixed amount per record index.

**Timestamps (irregular spacing)**
- The gaps between records are 44994, 54986, 49992, 44989, **5000**, 49990, 49989, 49990 and 39992.
- The gap between records 5 and 6 is about one-tenth of the others. Yet the jamming indicator and velocity still change by exactly the same amount across it.

## 3. Reasoning

**Evidence considered for Malicious**
- The exact linear progressions, constant even across a much shorter time gap, do not look like raw sensor behavior. Raw measurements usually contain noise. This pattern could in principle fit synthetic or injected values.
- The jamming indicator sits around 25–28 rather than near zero. However, its scale and meaning are not provided, so I cannot interpret this level as elevated or normal.
- A spoofing attack might show position drift while the fix quality stays good. Here the position drift is tiny and smooth, so this pattern is not clearly present.

**Evidence considered for Benign**
- Satellite count and fix type are constant. Nothing suggests degraded or disrupted reception within the window.
- Position and altitude change only by tiny, smooth amounts. No jumps occur, and nothing about the changes is physically implausible. The pattern is consistent with a near-stationary or slowly moving vehicle, matching the small, slowly changing velocity values.
- The jamming indicator is decreasing, not rising, across the window.
- The perfectly linear, index-based steps are better explained by a data-processing artifact than by an attack, though this is not established:
  - Linear interpolation or resampling between sparser source samples would produce constant per-record steps that ignore the actual timestamp gaps.
  - The piecewise-constant altitude steps also fit interpolation between successive source points.
  - These regularities therefore describe the dataset's construction. On their own, they do not indicate manipulation of the UAV's navigation.

**Decision**
- No feature shows a meaningful deviation that specifically supports malicious activity: no position jumps, no satellite or fix changes, no rising jamming trend, and no clear position–velocity inconsistency.
- The one unusual characteristic (exact linearity despite uneven timing) has a plausible processing explanation.
- On balance, the observations favor Benign. Because the window is short, units are unknown and no baseline is available, confidence is limited but not eliminated.

## 4. Alternative explanations

**Non-malicious explanations for the observed patterns**
- **Interpolation or upsampling:** Values may have been linearly interpolated between logged points, possibly from different source streams with different rates. This would explain the constant per-record steps and their independence from timestamp spacing.
- **Hover or slow drift:** The tiny position changes, slow altitude rise and low velocity are consistent with station-keeping or gentle movement.
- **Normal indicator variation:** The jamming indicator may simply vary within its normal operating range. Without its definition or scale, a value near 25–28 cannot be called anomalous.
- **Logging artifacts:** The short 5000-unit timestamp gap may reflect logging jitter or merging of message streams.

**The remaining malicious possibility**
- Exact linearity could also arise from fabricated or replayed data.
- Nothing in this window distinguishes that possibility from processing artifacts, so it remains a possibility rather than a supported conclusion.

## 5. Confidence: **Low**

- The absence of anomalies supports Benign.
- Confidence is reduced by:
  - the short window,
  - unknown units and feature definitions (especially `jamming_indicator`),
  - no baseline for the jamming level,
  - processing artifacts that may mask raw sensor behavior.

## 6. Additional evidence needed

- **Feature definitions:** the definition, scale and normal range of `jamming_indicator`, plus the units of the timestamps and position fields.
- **Raw data:** the original, non-interpolated telemetry and the preprocessing pipeline, to confirm whether the linear patterns come from resampling.
- **Baseline:** a longer window, or data from flights known to be benign, to compare jamming levels and drift behavior.
- **Additional GNSS quality metrics:** for example C/N0, HDOP/VDOP, noise level, spoofing flags and position accuracy estimates.
- **Cross-sensor checks:** comparison against inertial (IMU/accelerometer), barometric altitude or optical-flow data to test whether GNSS position and velocity agree with independent sensors.
- **Context:** flight mode or mission data (e.g., commanded hover) to confirm that near-stationary behavior was expected.
