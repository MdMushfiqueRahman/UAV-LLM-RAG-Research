# Telemetry Analysis: Sample S004

## 1. Classification
**Insufficient Evidence**

  - About 3.7–3.8e-7 per step through record 7.

**Timestamp intervals are irregular, but feature steps are not**
- The intervals are approximately 49988, 44990, 44989, 5001, 54984, 39995, 4996, 49987 and 54987.
- Two intervals (records 4→5 and 7→8) are about one-tenth the length of the others.

  - The jamming indicator moves by about 0.407 whether the gap is about 5000 or about 55000.
  - The lat/alt step change at record 8 follows a short interval, not a long one.

**Relationships between features**
- The jamming indicator and satellite count rise together.
- Fix type does not degrade.
- Position changes are small and smooth, and velocity changes only slightly.

## 3. Reasoning

**Evidence for Malicious**
- The jamming_indicator rises monotonically, by about 28% across the window.
- A sustained, steady rise in such an indicator could be consistent with growing RF interference.
- A smooth, low-rate position drift with a stable 3D fix is also a pattern sometimes discussed in GNSS spoofing.

**Evidence for Benign, or against an attack**
- No GNSS degradation co-occurs with the jamming rise:
  - Satellite count increases rather than decreases.
  - Fix type stays at 3.
  - This does not match the degradation commonly associated with jamming, though it does not rule out spoofing.
- Position, altitude and velocity change smoothly and modestly. No jumps or discontinuities appear.
- Without known units or a reference trajectory, the drift cannot be shown to diverge from true motion.

**Why the window does not discriminate between hypotheses**
- Every feature changes linearly by record index, regardless of the time interval between records.
- Satellites_used takes fractional values that step in exact ninths.
- These patterns strongly suggest the values were interpolated, resampled or smoothed during preprocessing, rather than being raw sensor readings.
- If so, the trends mainly reflect the processing, and fine-grained relationships cannot be reliably interpreted.

**Why the missing information matters here**
- The jamming_indicator's scale and normal range are not provided. A level of 13–17 cannot be judged as normal or elevated, and a rise of about 3.7 may or may not be meaningful.
- This is not simply a matter of a short window. The one feature pointing toward Malicious is uninterpretable without its scale, and the other features do not corroborate it.

## 4. Alternative explanations

- **Data processing:** linear interpolation or aggregation, such as averaging across time bins, would explain the fractional satellite counts, the constant per-record increments and the mismatch with timestamp spacing.
- **Normal environmental variation:** a jamming indicator can fluctuate with ambient RF noise, the UAV's own electronics, or changes in antenna orientation or position. Such variation may be benign.
- **Normal flight:** a slowly moving or hovering UAV with gradual position and altitude change and slight acceleration is consistent with ordinary low-speed flight or position-hold drift.
- **Normal satellite geometry:** an improving satellite count could reflect satellites rising above the horizon.

## 5. Confidence
**Medium** that the evidence does not meaningfully distinguish the two hypotheses.

There is low confidence in either Benign or Malicious specifically.

## 6. Additional evidence needed

- **Indicator definition:** the jamming_indicator's definition, units and baseline or threshold values for this platform.
- **Raw data:** unprocessed records, with integer satellite counts and true sample-by-sample values, plus documentation of any interpolation or resampling.
- **Units:** timestamp units, to check whether position change agrees with vel_m_s over the actual elapsed time.
- **Wider context:** a longer window, to see whether the jamming trend continues, plateaus or reverses, and how it compares with known-benign flights.
- **Additional GNSS quality metrics:** carrier-to-noise density (C/N0), HDOP/VDOP, noise level, AGC and spoofing-detection flags.
- **Independent cross-checks:** IMU, barometer, optical flow or visual odometry, compared against the GNSS position, velocity and altitude, to detect any divergence consistent with spoofing.
- **Ground truth:** the flight plan or commanded trajectory, to compare against the reported positions.
